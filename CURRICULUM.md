# Learning Quest: curriculum map

For every level: the learning outcome, the success criteria the child sees ("I can…"), and its link to the NCF for Foundational Stage 2022 (NCF-FS), the NCERT Learning Outcomes (2017), the NCERT Class 2 textbooks and, for Tamil, the topics of the Tamil Nadu Samacheer Kalvi Class 2 Tamil course. NCF-FS and NCERT codes and quotes were checked against the official PDFs. "Confidence" shows how closely each level matches.

Sources: [NCF for Foundational Stage 2022 (NCERT)](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf) · [NCERT Learning Outcomes at the Elementary Stage (2017)](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/otherpublications/tilops101.pdf) · [NIPUN Bharat guidelines](https://dsel.education.gov.in/nipun-bharat) · [Chrysalis Thinkers](https://chrysalis.world/products/thinkers) · [Chrysalis ThinkRoom](https://chrysalis.world/products/thinkroom) · [LEGO Foundation: Learning through play (2017)](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/media/316884)

## How the game teaches

- **Learning goal and success criteria.** Every level opens with today's goal and two 'I can…' statements, so the child knows what success looks like. Chrysalis lesson plans also state learning outcomes and success criteria. *In the game:* The goal card appears the first two times a level is played. The 10 questions check exactly those criteria.
- **Chrysalis lesson flow.** Chrysalis describes lessons that move through engagement, concept-building, assessment and reflection. *In the game:* Every level runs as a conversation with Kili in five steps. 1 Wonder (engagement): a story world and the learning goal. 2 Think together (concept-building): Kili solves an example out loud with a picture and a strategy. 3 Play (assessment): 10 questions checked against the success criteria, with 'How did you work that out?' thinking triggers. 4 Brain buster: an optional harder bonus question. 5 Teach Kili and 'How did that feel?' (expression and reflection).
- **Choice, wonder and joy (NCF-FS 1.4.1).** Play built on choice, wonder and joy. *In the game:* The child chooses the subject and level, earns stars, coins and stickers, and hears praise for effort as well as for right answers.
- **Guided play and scaffolding (NCF-FS 1.4.2).** The child leads while an adult, here the game, gently scaffolds toward a goal. *In the game:* A first wrong answer brings a hint with a strategy, not the answer. A second wrong answer shows the answer, then the game moves on.
- **Try, test and retry.** Learning through trial and error without fear of failing. *In the game:* There's no timer and no penalty for mistakes. Wrong answers are recycled into the 'Practise tricky ones' round, and the child hears that mistakes help us learn.
- **Concrete, then pictorial, then abstract.** Ideas are first shown with objects and pictures before symbols. *In the game:* Early levels in each world use pictures (dots, rods and cubes, coins, clock faces, objects). Later levels use numbers and words alone.
- **Home language as a bridge (NCF-FS 3.2).** Using the child's home language and context helps when learning in an unfamiliar language. *In the game:* Instructions, hints and praise are spoken in Tamil, while English and Hindi words are spoken in their own voices. The contexts are Indian: rupees, Pongal, idli and the auto rickshaw.
- **Mastery before moving on.** Children move ahead when they show they've learned, not by the clock. *In the game:* The next level unlocks at 7 of 10 right on the first try. Parents can unlock all levels.
- **Express and reflect.** Chrysalis: 'Learning is incomplete if students aren't confident to express what they know.' *In the game:* Kili listens after every question, so the child can answer, ask for help, say 'I don't know' or ask Kili to repeat. At the end the child teaches Kili what they learned and rates the level Easy, OK or Tricky. Parents see these ratings in the daily log.
- **Sensible screen time (NCF-FS 5.4.7).** Screens should support, not replace, real play and conversation. *In the game:* Short 10-question levels and a daily goal. The dashboard suggests the tricky questions to talk through together, away from the screen.

## Maths

All 40 Maths levels map to NCF-FS CG-8 (competencies C-8.2 to C-8.13). The NCERT 2017 Class II Mathematics outcomes cover two-digit numbers, place value, addition and subtraction, money up to Rs. 100, shapes, non-standard measurement, and days and months. Joyful Mathematics 2 has clear chapters for most worlds. Several levels sit below Class 2 (single-digit facts, counting to 20) and work as warm-ups. Some sit above it or outside it: cm with a ruler, half past, odd/even, and the table of 4 as a standalone table.

## English

For a Tamil-speaking child, English is a second language (L2). Phonics and reading levels therefore map to NCF-FS CG-11 (C-11.1, C-11.2), and oral and vocabulary levels map to CG-9 (C-9.x). NCERT's own 'About the Book' for Mridang 2 maps that book to C-9.1 to C-9.7 and C-11.1. The NCERT 2017 Class II English outcomes match the describing-words, pronoun, sentence-writing and comprehension levels exactly. They say nothing about phonics, plurals or nouns. NIPUN Bharat Class II covers naming words, action words and punctuation.

## Hindi

In a Tamil-speaking home, Hindi is the child's L2 or L3, not L1. NCF-FS expects a less familiar language to be at an emergent level by the end of Grade 2 (footnote to CG-9; Section 3.2), so the Hindi levels map mainly to CG-11 (C-11.1, C-11.2) and CG-9 (C-9.x). The NCERT 2017 Class II Hindi outcomes assume Hindi as a school language. The letter and matra levels fit their akshara, sound and print statements. Sarangi 2 matches some levels: aa-matra colour words (ch. 8), opposites (ch. 25) and the colours unit (Unit 2). Note: the NCERT LO PDF's Hindi text layer mis-orders some Devanagari glyphs.
